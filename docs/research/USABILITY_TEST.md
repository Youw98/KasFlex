# Ten-minute grower usability test

This is a short, repeatable test for the default KasFlex interface. It tests
whether a grower can understand the decision without a software walkthrough. It
does not test whether the greenhouse model is accurate.

## Who and how

- Recruit at least five people who operate or advise a commercial greenhouse.
- Prefer participants who have not seen KasFlex before.
- Use a laptop or desktop at normal browser zoom, in Dutch unless the participant
  normally works in English.
- Read the introduction below verbatim. Do not explain controls or vocabulary
  while the timer is running.
- Record only with the participant's explicit consent. Anonymous use must remain
  fully functional.

Introduction:

> This screen proposes tomorrow's energy plan. Please think aloud. I am testing the
> software, not you. I cannot help during the tasks, but you may stop at any time.

## Tasks (ten minutes total)

| Time | Task | Success evidence |
|---:|---|---|
| 0:00 | Find tomorrow's expected cost and simulated tomato outcome. | States both values and notices that tomato growth is a model output. |
| 1:30 | Explain whether the independent safety check is on and what switching it off means. | Identifies the switch and says an unchecked plan cannot be finally approved. |
| 3:00 | Say that the money side is acceptable but the crop side is not. | Responds to money and crop separately; does not reject the whole plan. |
| 5:00 | Ask KasFlex for a crop-specific alternative and explain its trade-off. | Finds the targeted response and can restate what changed. |
| 7:00 | Open the energy-position screen and find one hour with a short or long position. | Opens the dedicated screen and identifies the direction and settlement. |
| 8:30 | Return and determine what is still needed before approval. | Understands the four-part progress and checker gate. |

Stop at ten minutes even if tasks remain unfinished.

## What to record

For each task record completion (`yes`, `with prompting`, `no`), elapsed time,
wrong turns, and the participant's exact words for anything unclear. Also record:

- whether money and tomato information were found first;
- whether “position”, “settlement”, “DLI”, “CO₂”, “bad-weather case” or “checker”
  needed explanation;
- whether the participant confused measured data with simulated results;
- whether separate money/crop/grid/work responses felt useful or repetitive;
- the first thing the participant would remove from the page.

Do not turn a facilitator explanation into a successful completion.

## Exit questions

Ask these after the timer:

1. In your own words, what will this plan do tomorrow?
2. Which number would you trust least, and why?
3. What does the safety check protect you from?
4. Could you disagree with the crop effect without losing the money decision?
5. Which term sounded like project jargon rather than grower language?
6. Would you use this in a morning planning meeting? What blocks you?

## Acceptance thresholds

The interface is ready for a wider demonstration only if:

- at least 4 of 5 participants finish tasks 1–5 within ten minutes without help;
- all participants can distinguish simulated tomato output from measured facts;
- at least 4 of 5 understand that checker-off is a demonstration, not an
  approvable operating mode;
- at least 4 of 5 complete a crop-only disagreement without rejecting the full
  plan; and
- no critical term is misunderstood by more than one participant.

Any safety-check misunderstanding is a release blocker. Reword the interface,
repeat with new participants, and keep both rounds in the study log.
